Privacy policy

Acolyte for Android, iPhone, Apple TV and Android TV · Effective 25 November 2026

Short version: Acolyte has no servers of its own and the developer receives no data from the app. This page says what the app does on your device and the few places it can reach besides your own server.

In short

  • The app talks to the HolyArrs server address you enter. The developer runs no server and receives nothing.
  • No analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising and no tracking code is built into the app.
  • Your sign-in and a few small preferences are stored on your device in the Android Keystore or the Apple Keychain.
  • A few optional features can reach other sites, for example trailers. Each one is listed in section 5.
  • Your server and whoever runs it are outside this policy.

1. Who is responsible

Acolyte is developed by Orell Bühler, an individual in Switzerland. Acolyte is an independent app for the self-hosted media server HolyArrs. It is not made by, and not connected to, the HolyArrs project.

This policy is written with the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection (FADP) and the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) in mind. It describes what the app does; it makes no further legal promises.

2. What I collect

Nothing. The app has no backend of the developer. It contains no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising and no tracking library, and it does not read an advertising identifier. I do not receive your server address, your account, what you watch or ask for, your IP address, or anything else from the app.

The app does not need an account with me. You sign in to your own server, nowhere else.

Two things sit outside the app and are not mine: the Apple App Store, TestFlight and Google Play handle downloads under their own terms, and they may show me anonymous, aggregated statistics (for example downloads, or crash reports from people who allowed their device to share diagnostics). The app itself contains no code that sends such data. GitHub handles anything you post there (see Contact).

3. What goes to your server

Everything the app does goes over the network to the address you entered when you connected. That includes:

  • your user name and password when you sign in (the password is sent to sign in and is not stored by the app), or your API key;
  • the requests behind every screen: browsing, searching, asking for titles, settings, and so on;
  • files you choose yourself, such as a profile picture, torrent files or a backup file you restore;
  • a user-agent line with the app version and platform, in the form Acolyte/<version> (Android).

While a screen is open, the app also listens for live updates from your server. Your sign-in cookie or API key is attached only to requests to your own server, never to requests to other sites.

If you enter an address without https://, the app tries HTTPS first and then plain HTTP. Plain HTTP is not encrypted: use it only on a network you trust, and use HTTPS for a server you reach over the internet. On iPhone and Apple TV, plain HTTP is only allowed for local network addresses.

4. What is stored on your device

The app stores the following on your device only. None of it is sent to me.

WhatWhyWhereHow to remove it
Server address, your user name, how you signed in To reconnect and to fill in the sign-in screen next time Secure store (see below) Uninstall, or clear the app data (Android)
Session cookie or API key, and the security token for requests that change something To keep you signed in Secure store Sign out in the app
A copy of your account details from the server: name, role, permissions, which parts of the server are on, display name, language, and the server version if you manage the server So the app opens at once and refreshes in the background Secure store Sign out in the app
Small preferences: saved library filters, the last library tab, Explore and search choices, recent searches Convenience Secure store Uninstall, or clear the app data (Android). Signing out keeps them.
Pictures you have seen (posters, backdrops, avatars) Speed The app's cache; on iPhone and Apple TV up to 200 MB on disk Clear the cache or storage, or uninstall; the system also trims it
Temporary files when you export, download or share something To hand the file to the share sheet The app's cache or temporary folder The app clears them after sharing

The secure store. On Android, each value is encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a key that lives in the Android Keystore and never leaves it; the encrypted values sit in the app's private storage. On iPhone and Apple TV the values are in the Keychain, readable after the first unlock and only on this device: they are not synced through iCloud Keychain and do not move to another device.

Backups. Android Auto Backup is switched on for the app but every kind of app data is excluded, so nothing from the app goes to Google's cloud backup, and the secure store is excluded from device-to-device transfer. After restoring or switching phones you sign in again.

Deleting it. Signing out removes your credentials, the cached account details and the security token, and asks your server to end the session (unless you used an API key: then the key stays valid on the server until someone deletes it there). It keeps the server address, your user name and the small preferences. On Android, uninstalling the app or clearing its storage removes everything. On iPhone and Apple TV, iOS may keep Keychain items after an app is deleted, so the remaining entries (server address, user name, preferences, but no password or key after you signed out) can still be there if you reinstall. Other apps cannot read them.

5. Other sites the app can reach

By default the app talks only to your server. These are the other places it can reach, and when. The app sends none of your credentials to any of them.

WhoWhenWhat they can see
YouTube (Google), i.ytimg.com When a title page shows trailer cards, or an artist page shows music videos. The app loads the small preview picture directly from YouTube. Your IP address, which video the picture belongs to, and the usual details of a web request.
YouTube video servers (Google) When you play a trailer. Your server finds the trailer and gives the app a stream address on YouTube's servers; the app plays it directly. If that fails, the app tries your server's relay, and if that fails too it opens the YouTube page in your browser or the YouTube app. Accounts your server marks as kids, and titles without a TMDB entry, only get the YouTube link. Your IP address and the video request. If the YouTube page opens, YouTube's own policy applies.
Websites you open: IMDb, TMDB, Trakt, Letterboxd; HolyArrs documentation and GitHub pages; TMDB, TheTVDB, MDBList and fanart.tv key pages; HolyArrs sponsor pages; Plex sign-in Only when you tap such a link. They open in your browser or another app, outside Acolyte. Most are in title pages or in the server settings for managers. The address you open (for a title link it contains the title's ID) and whatever your browser sends. Their own policies apply.
Addresses your server supplies Your server can give the app full web addresses: release notes, the API reference, a single sign-on provider, a Plex sign-in page or a picture hosted elsewhere. The app opens links only when you tap them. A picture address on another site is loaded from that site directly. The site behind the address: your IP address and the request.

Posters and backdrops. TMDB picture addresses are rewritten to your server's own image cache while you are signed in, so TMDB does not see your device. Only pictures that your server gives as a full address on another site (and the YouTube previews above) come directly from that site, without credentials.

I do not control these sites and cannot promise what they do. If you do not want the YouTube requests, do not open title pages with trailers or tap trailers.

6. Permissions

PlatformPermissionPurpose
Android Internet To reach your server and the sites in section 5.
iPhone and Apple TV Local network iOS and tvOS ask before an app talks to devices on your home network. The app says: “Acolyte connects to your HolyArrs server on your local network.” If you decline, a server on your home network cannot be reached.

The app does not ask for access to your location, contacts, camera, microphone, notifications, photo library or general file storage, and does not use the tracking permission. Choosing a profile picture, a backup file or torrent files uses the system's own picker: the app only receives what you pick. Files you export are handed to the system share sheet.

7. Your server and its operator

Acolyte shows what your server tells it. The server and the person who runs it are not part of this policy. The operator can see who signs in, from which address, and what is browsed, asked for, downloaded and changed, and keeps whatever their own setup keeps. If the server is run by someone else, such as a friend, ask them. The HolyArrs software has its own documentation and policies.

8. Children

Acolyte is not aimed at children and collects no personal data from anyone, children included. If a child uses the app, the account and its limits (for example a kids profile) are set by the operator of the server.

9. Your rights

Because I hold no personal data about you, there is nothing for me to look up, correct or delete. Everything the app stores is on your device and you can remove it as described in section 4. For data on your server, ask its operator. If you believe your data protection rights have been breached, you can also contact the data protection authority where you live; in Switzerland that is the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC).

10. Changes to this policy

If the app starts to do something that changes this page, I will update the page before that version is released and change the date at the top. Earlier versions are in the public history of the source code on GitHub.

11. Contact

For privacy questions, open an issue at github.com/OrellBuehler/acolyte/issues. Issues there are public, so do not put passwords, API keys or private server addresses in them. If something has to stay private, say so in the issue and I will arrange another way.
